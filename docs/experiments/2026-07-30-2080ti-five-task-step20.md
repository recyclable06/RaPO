# RTX 2080 Ti 20-step five-task comparison (2026-07-30)

## Scope and status

Status: **passed with compatibility deviations** for a five-task GRPO/RaPO
engineering gate on 8 x RTX 2080 Ti. Both method chains completed task-5
training, save, reload, and the same fixed evaluation. The bounded comparison
continues to favor GRPO. It is not a paper-level result because it uses one
class order, one run, 20 optimizer steps per task, and a 20-image-per-class
evaluation subset.

Task-5 training used RaPO repository commit `ba77ef5` and the pinned Visual-RFT
commit `2ffad63b25ddd79bfe25d3e046645401201c89d6`. The data, model, and
optimization settings match the four-task gate except for two shared 2080 Ti
compatibility controls: cuDNN was disabled with `RAPO_DISABLE_CUDNN=1`, and the
DeepSpeed dynamic FP16 initial loss scale was lowered from 2,048 to 1,024.
These controls were applied identically to GRPO and RaPO.

## Compatibility investigation

The first task-5 RaPO attempt reproduced `CUDNN_STATUS_INTERNAL_ERROR` during
the first backward pass on two clean launches. Switching PyTorch to the cuDNN
v7 compatibility path also failed at the same point. Disabling cuDNN allowed
training to proceed, but the original FP16 scale produced an early skipped
optimizer update. That partial run was stopped before saving a model.

The successful configuration disabled cuDNN and used the lower initial FP16
scale. Both final runs completed all 20 expected learning-rate values, from
`9.5e-7` through `0`, with no skipped update, OOM, NaN, or traceback. The
failed logs are retained remotely with the `failed-cudnn1`, `failed-cudnn2`,
`failed-cudnn-v7`, and `failed-fp16-skip` suffixes.

The dominant task-1 asymmetry persists exactly: every RaPO-correct task-1
example is also correct for GRPO. Representative new confusions include
`axolotl` becoming `jellyfish` or `whippet`, `hermit crab` becoming `tarantula`
or `snail`, and `american egret` becoming `goose`. Conversely, RaPO's small
task-4 advantage and near-matched task-5 accuracy show that the chain still
learns recent tasks. No ordered-key mismatch, duplicate sample, missing answer,
evaluation crash, or unsaved final model explains the gap.

## Decision

The five-task engineering gate passes, but the repeated gap activates the
predeclared audit condition. Do not interpret this short single-order result as
a refutation or reproduction of the paper. Before extending the chain through
task 6, re-check the paper equations against the trainer-level rollout,
reference-policy, reward, distributed CTAN, and task-transition paths. Continue
only if that audit finds no material mismatch or after any mismatch is fixed
and tested.
